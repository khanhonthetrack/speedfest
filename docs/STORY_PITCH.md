# Story mode: three premises (M20-1, the pitch)

For the owner to pick one, mix them, or redirect, before any script is written
(`docs/STORY_MODE_PROMPT.md`, section 3). All three are new: nothing from the rejected "Rear View"
draft. Each one is set in the neon City at night, uses ten nights, three maps a night, and a 3-5-way
decision taken by the finish line you cross on the third map. Each also keeps your session-11 ending
(beat the last rival, push them off, they crash, you walk up and take their car) as **one** of its
endings, never the only one.

Each premise was checked against the brief's test for a story that plays in any order:
- it asks a question rather than running a countdown;
- the whole cast is there on every night;
- every night has its own goal plus one hard fact;
- it is told in cars, not in rooms;
- every night ends in a real fork, with the cruel choice on the fast line.

How the any-order part works, for all three: every night's fact is a separate record of one
night in the past, so the facts only add up and never contradict. Dialogue comes in three
versions, picked by how much the player already knows, not by chapter number. A frame in the
story itself makes the shuffled order feel intended.

## A. PREVIOUS OWNER

**The pitch.** You buy a cheap black coupe at a night auction on the docks, and the whole city
recognises it. It is Vesper's car: the street legend's white coupe that went off the Halloran toll
bridge eight years ago and was pulled out of the river empty. You are Kit, a night courier. The car
came with its old satnav still bolted in: nine unmarked pins on the city map, no route between them,
the bridge greyed out. Each night you drive to one pin (a salvage yard, a car-park roof, the closed
toll plaza, a body shop, a noodle bar, a river lock, a cemetery hill, a police patrol, a night
market) and each pin gives you one hard fact about the bridge. Who raced Vesper that night? Who
raised the barrier? Who paid to close the case? And why did somebody make sure this car ended up
with you? The pins have no order, because Vesper "never drove anywhere the same way twice". The last
night is the bridge itself, in rain, against the Halloran heir in his silver hypercar, and the
person waiting at the rail is the last one you would expect.

- **The question:** who was Vesper, and what happened on the Halloran Bridge?
- **The frame:** Vesper's satnav is the chapter board. A pin turns red when its fact is learned, your
  driven lines trace over the map, and on every third map each finish gate faces a different pin.
  Dee, your mechanic at Arch 9 under the railway viaduct, reads the satnav back to you at the start
  of every night.
- **You drive:** the black widebody coupe with violet underglow (the Nemesis GT in Black Ops, the car
  already on the menu's Story picture), the same car all story long.
- **Cast:** Dee Sandoval (your mechanic, never sits in a driver's seat: "lost my licence, long
  story"); Cass Halloran (heir to the toll-road family, silver hypercar); Edgar Halloran (his father,
  never seen outside his black V12 grand tourer); Sgt. Lena Voss (traffic cop, first at the bridge,
  taken off the case in 36 hours); Moth (19, runs the roof meets and the WHERE IS VESPER channel);
  Gus Pell (salvage yard, pulled the car out of the river); Pim (noodle bar, has held an envelope
  "for Vesper" for eight years).
- **The nights:** the Yard (the chassis stamp under the black paint matches the car from the river),
  the Roof (Vesper raced one driver only, Cass, 31 times, on the bridge at 3 a.m.), the Plaza (on the
  last night two cars went through Lane 3 nose to tail, and the barrier was raised from the Halloran
  control room for a silver car), the Body Shop (Cass's silver saloon came in next morning with white
  paint in the dent), the Noodle Strip (Vesper's last bet: the keys of Halloran Holdings, signed by
  Edgar), the Lock (a soaked woman climbed the lock ladder at 03:40: Vesper lived), the Hill (Edgar's
  daughter Ines "died abroad" that week; her photo shows her leaning on a white coupe with violet
  underglow), the Patrol (the rail was rammed at 190 km/h, and Edgar phoned the commissioner), the
  Market (the satnav was built after the bridge, its pins loaded five weeks ago, and the auction was
  rigged so the car fell to you), then the Bridge.
- **A decision (the Noodle Strip):** Edgar's driver wants Pim's envelope. Gates: sell it to him for the
  cash in his glovebox (the cold one: the fastest line, through the market crossing against the
  lights); give it to Dee; give it to Sgt. Voss; burn it in Pim's stove so nobody comes for Pim again.
- **The twist (spoiler):** Dee is Vesper. She is Ines Halloran, Cass's sister, who climbed out of the
  river; her father had her declared dead rather than let her put his son in prison. She rebuilt her
  own car, painted it black, loaded the pins and rigged the auction so it would fall to a courier
  whose run passes Halloran Tower at 3 a.m., so her brother would see her car every night until he
  broke. Every clue has an innocent reading: she never drives ("lost my licence"), she fitted violet
  underglow ("it's what sells"), she knows the white car's paint code by heart ("paint nerd"), she
  once calls Cass "Cassie" (mocking a rich boy). Red herring: Sgt. Voss, the woman who knows the
  bridge too well and keeps letting you go.
- **Endings:** VESPER (the inside rail: you run Cass into the toll barrier, walk up through the rain
  and take the hypercar and the company keys; the city starts calling you Vesper; your old ending),
  INES (you beat him clean or stop at the rail; Dee says her own name into the sergeant's radio;
  last scene, the coupe painted white again with Dee in the driver's seat for the first time in eight
  years), SALVAGE (you take the money or lose Dee's trust; the coupe is crushed while you watch through
  the fence). Secret: WHITE (all nine pins and full trust: Vesper drives her own car against her
  brother for the 32nd time).
- **Why any order:** every fact is a record or a witness of one night eight years ago; the present
  never moves (Dee at Arch 9, Cass in his tower, Moth on his roof); decisions change who trusts you,
  not what happened.
- **Shot example:** floodlights on the docks, rain; a crane lowers the black coupe off a barge; a
  hooded figure raises a bidding paddle, seen from behind; inside, the dash wakes and the satnav
  boots: nine violet pins; under the arches, Dee's silhouette behind welding sparks: "So. You bought
  a ghost."
- **Tone:** *Drive*, *Blade Runner 2049*, the NFSU2 intro. Violet.
- **Risks:** a mentor who turns out to be the hidden one is a familiar shape, so Dee has to be
  genuinely likeable and Voss a real suspect.

## B. STATIC HOUR

**The pitch.** Twelve years ago, Glass Night, an illegal race through the Ninth Street underpass,
killed three people, and your brother Dario went to prison for it. You were eight. Tonight a caller
on *Static Hour*, a 2-to-5 a.m. radio phone-in, says Dario didn't do it: "Ask about the grey car."
The host, June, lost her husband that night, and she opens the lines for a month. You are Remi,
twenty, a delivery driver who lives above your mum's 24-hour laundrette and drives a dented white hot
hatch. Each night is one caller: the impound where Dario's car is about to be crushed, a promoter's
spiral car park, a stolen taxi with an old dashcam, the underpass itself, a scrapyard ledger, a
paramedic's run, the prison road across the marsh, the docks, the radio mast on the hill. Each gives
one piece of the same three seconds. Nobody saw it in order, so June takes the calls as they come,
and at the end of every night she tells the whole city what "our driver" found, while your mum folds
sheets with the radio on. On the last night the underpass reopens for a memorial race against the man
who ran Glass Night, live on air, and the answer is standing in the slip road.

- **The question:** what was the grey car on Glass Night?
- **The frame:** the radio show. The switchboard's nine lines are the chapter board; every night opens
  with June on air (a silhouette in the lit booth, rain on the glass, the ON AIR lamp red) and closes
  with her telling the city what you found, so your choices are heard by your rivals and your family.
  On every third map the finish gates are the lines still holding.
- **You drive:** Remi's dented white three-door hot hatch, all story long.
- **Cast:** June Okoro (the host, widow of the race marshal who died); Dario (your brother, rings from
  prison at 1 a.m., begs you to stop); Marisol (your mum, runs the laundrette, hasn't driven in twelve
  years); Augustin Kade (ran Glass Night, owns the spiral car park and every meet, silver V8 GT); Pike
  Varga (drove the car that hit Dario's, hasn't walked since, muscle car on hand controls); Hal
  Brennan (the cop who "lost" the tape); Nell Achebe (the paramedic who got there first).
- **The nights:** the Impound (Dario's logger: 1.1 s before impact he was at full brake and full lock;
  he swerved), the Spiral (Kade filmed the whole night and has the only copy), the Cab (a dashcam: the
  grey car was standing across the slip road with its hazards on: blocking, not racing), the
  Underpass (racing Pike's ghost through the tunnel he crashed in; and under those old sodium lamps
  every colour reads grey), the River Yard (two days later a dark compact sedan was scrapped for cash,
  a child seat in the back), the Ambulance Bay (the first person at the wreck was a woman in a
  cardigan who said Dario's name and vanished), Saltmarsh (Dario pleaded guilty six hours later, right
  after a visit from "family"), the Docks (the cop erased the tape for Kade: "keep the slip road out of
  it"), the Needle (the marshal died on foot, running to wave a car back), then the Last Line.
- **A decision (the Cab):** a fifteen-year-old has stolen the taxi with the dashcam. Gates: let the kid
  go; hand him to the patrol; box him into the barrier and stop him dead (the cruel one, the fastest
  gap line); give him Kade's number, because Kade pays kids like him.
- **The twist (spoiler):** the grey car was green. It was your mum's old sedan, turned grey by the
  sodium lamps. She drove to the underpass to stop Dario racing and parked across the slip road with
  you asleep in the back seat. Dario swerved to miss his mother and his little sibling, the field
  piled into him, and June's husband died running to wave her away. Dario confessed to protect her.
  The anonymous caller was Mum. Clues, each innocent alone: "I'll stand in the road before I let him
  race"; your one happy memory of "the only time Mum drove at night, tunnel lights going over like a
  zipper"; the sedan "sold to pay Dario's lawyer"; a scrapped car with a child seat (which seems to
  point at the marshal's own family).
- **Endings:** GLASS (the wall line: you put Kade into the tiles where the field piled up, walk up, take
  the tape and his silver GT, and burn the tape while Mum watches; nobody ever knows; your old ending),
  GREEN (you give the tape to Mum, not to the radio; at 4 a.m. she walks into the station and gives her
  name on air; months later you pick Dario up at the prison gate and the city's lights go over the car
  like a zipper), STATIC (you play the tape live without her consent; the whole strip watches her
  arrested; Dario never calls again; you alone in the hatch, the dial on static).
- **Why any order:** all nine facts are separate observations of the same three seconds; each has an
  innocent reading alone; June is on air every night, Dario rings at 1 a.m., Mum is in the laundrette.
- **Shot example:** the radio mast on the hill in rain; June's silhouette in the lit booth; cut to the
  laundrette window, Mum folding sheets, the radio on; Remi's hatch idling at the kerb, wipers going;
  the caller's words in subtitles over the underpass hoardings, lit one colour of orange.
- **Tone:** *Collateral*, *As Dusk Falls*, late-night radio. Sodium orange.
- **Risks:** heavy (three deaths, a mother's guilt), so it needs restraint; the sodium clue must stay a
  throwaway or sharp players guess early.

## C. TEN KEYS

**The pitch.** You are Teo, a night tow-truck driver who co-signed the loan for your sister Lina's
noodle bar. The loan went bad, the shark sold it on, and now someone called the Ring owns your debt.
An envelope arrives: a steel ring of ten keys, nine tagged with places in the night city, one with no
tag, and a text: "A debt doesn't care what order you pay it in." Each key is a car stashed somewhere
(a hotel's valet basement, a tow yard, the railway arches, a market roof, a container port, a car
wash, a ring-road services, the police impound, a bank's loading bay). Get it out past whoever guards
it and deliver it, and the tab drops by 6,000. Lina chalks the new figure behind her till every
night. Every night you drive a different car, and every drop-off is a choice: deliver it, give it
back to the people it was taken from, hand it to the detective, or take the fast, cruel line for
double. Every car also hides a loan shark's book of names. The question is who bought your debt, and
why everything points at the developer who wants your street. The tenth key opens the noodle bar's
back room.

- **The question:** who bought my debt?
- **The frame:** the keyring and the tab. Every night opens at the key kiosk under the railway
  arches, where you pick a hook (the chapter board in the story's own world); every night closes at
  Lina's counter, where the Ring's text reads your night back to you ("Key home. You took the river
  line. Burned what wasn't yours. Tab: 30,000.") and Lina chalks the figure. The finish gates are
  drop-offs.
- **You drive:** your own grey hatch on map 1 of every night, then that night's keyed car: a different
  one of the Garage's sixteen road cars each night, fixed per night so the times stay fair.
- **Cast:** Lina (your sister, runs the Lantern, a noodle bar that never has customers); Pell (70, the
  key-cutter under the arches); Mira Ostrova (owns every car park in the city, wants your street paved,
  long dark coat, pearl-white saloon and a fleet of white vans); Arlo Brigg (the shark who sold your
  debt and regrets it); DS Ayo Fen (fraud detective, wants you as an informant); Jody (your ex, unlocks
  phones at the night market); the Duc brothers (run the hand car wash under the flyover).
- **The nights:** the Meridian (all nine cars were bought at one auction by a company with a PO box on
  your street, where Ostrova has her office), Brigg's Yard (the Ring bought your debt for a sixth of
  its value, cash in a noodle-bar takeaway bag), the Arches (the keys were cut for a woman in a long
  dark coat who paid in coins), the Night Market (the Ring's phone only ever pings your street's mast,
  2-5 a.m.), the Port (each car hides a shark's ledger: the Ring is collecting the city's debt books),
  the Car Wash (every debt on your street was bought by the same buyer), the Services (the Ring paid
  the shark to offer your sister the loan in the first place), the Impound (the cars were collected by
  a white van), the Bank (the Ring's account takes 3,000 small card payments a month, 2-5 a.m., 6.50,
  8.90, 11.20), then the Lantern.
- **A decision (the Car Wash):** the Duc brothers are washing a car the Ring now owns. Gates: deliver it;
  tell them who sent you; leave them the car and burn their IOU; or the cold, fast line, out through
  the closing roller door with the elder brother's hands still on the bonnet.
- **The twist (spoiler):** the Ring is the street. Lina and old Pell organised Arkwright Street's
  shopkeepers to buy their own debts, and the sharks' books that hold half the city's, before Ostrova
  could buy them and foreclose. They laundered it through their own tills: 6.50, 8.90 and 11.20 are
  the street's own prices, on the chalk boards you pass every night. They needed a driver with a clean
  licence who couldn't say no, so Lina arranged your debt. Everyone you helped or hurt on the way (the
  car-wash brothers, the stallholders, the hotel porter) was one of your employers. And every camera
  has your face: if it goes wrong, the city's Ring is the tow-truck driver.
- **Endings:** THE RING (the barrier's maintenance gap: you shoulder Ostrova's white saloon into the
  spillway, walk down through the rain, take her car and drive it back to your street; the Ring's
  phone is yours now; your old ending), OPEN ALL NIGHT (you threw enough books in the river along the
  way: at dawn the Lantern's shutters go up for real and its first customers are the people you let
  off), CLEARED (you drive Lina into the police pound; your debt never existed; you owe nothing and
  have no one). Secret: NINEFOLD (you kept copies of the books, and you are the next key-cutter).
- **Why any order:** a debt is a sum, and subtraction doesn't care about order; each key is a separate
  job with its own guard; each fact is a separate record of one purchase made before the first night.
- **Shot example:** the lit key kiosk under the arches, a train thundering overhead; a hand lifts a
  tagged key off its hook; steam on the noodle bar's window, Lina's silhouette chalking the tab; a phone
  lights up on the counter: "Key home. Tab: 30,000. Five hooks empty."
- **Tone:** *Heat*, *Baby Driver* at night, *Uncut Gems*. Red neon and white vans.
- **Risks:** nine fetch jobs could feel samey, so every night needs its own guard and set piece; the
  chalk prices are the fairest clue and must stay soft behind the steam.

## Also written: ACCOUNT ELEVEN (not one of the three)

Nadia, 23, hasn't lost a race at the Deck in eight months, and every race carried a winning bet from
an account with no name. The money behind the book has lost 1.2 million and has decided it must be
her: nine rivals on the bookie's board, raced in any order, one question: who is betting on me? (The
answer is her father, once the book's fixer. Left out because it rhymes with B's parent twist and
because "your wins were bought" turns the player's own racing against them; available if wanted.)

## Questions for the owner (with what I'd do)

1. **Which premise?** A, B or C, a mix, or none of them. I'd take **B** for the strongest twist and
   endings, or **A** for the strongest use of the cars and the city.
2. **The ending:** your session-11 beats (push the last rival, crash, walk up, take the car) are one
   ending in each premise. Keep them that way? How many endings: I'd do three plus a secret one.
3. **How free is the order?** With one fixed first night and the finish alone deciding, the draft graph
   gives 294 different orders, and every night can come at 6-7 different places. A board where you can
   also pick any unplayed night gives every order. I'd go with the finish deciding, the board only when
   a map's exits are all played, and the first night fixed.
4. **The finale** opens after how many of the other nine? I'd say **7**, so a player can reach the end
   having missed two nights, and replay for them.
5. **Difficulty:** Cruise x1.30, Street x1.18, Racer x1.08, Author x1.00 of the bot's author time.
   Changeable between nights?
6. **Finishes on map 3:** I'd use 4 almost everywhere (three story choices plus the cold fast one), 3
   or 5 where a night needs it.
7. **The car:** fixed per premise (A and B: one car all story long; C: a different car each night),
   never carried over from a win, so the times stay fair. OK?
8. **People:** kept in silhouette (the animation library's mannequin, dressed dark, plus Quaternius'
   free base bodies and hair for distinct shapes), or do you want a modelled cast?
9. **Scope:** night 1 first as a playable slice (three maps, its scenes, its decision), for you to play
   before the other nine are made?
